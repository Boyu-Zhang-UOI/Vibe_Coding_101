## 1. New research on learning programming with AI (novices, non-programmers, vibe coding)

### Takeaway
Research published since the baseline confirms its central finding and strengthens it. **AI raises performance on the task in front of the student but lowers what the student can recall, explain or do once the AI is taken away.** Several new controlled studies show this (Bergh et al. [W]; Sankaranarayanan [E]; Gardella et al. ICER 2026 [E]). The strongest new evidence is negative: a 2,379-student RCT found that a course-integrated AI tutor *lowered* final grades by 0.37 SD, with larger losses for first-generation students [W]. Interventions that add friction or require explanation help: an explanation gate, oral code-review interviews, and deliberately planted bugs all had positive results. Writing skill and CS knowledge predict who vibe codes well. There is still **no semester-long RCT of teaching non-programmers through vibe coding**, and no human-subjects test of spec-first or test-first teaching.

### Cited Findings

**Randomized and controlled studies**
- **[W] Course-integrated AI tutor RCT, 2,379 undergraduates, 30 instructors, "a large U.S. public university"** (EdWorkingPaper 26-1598, posted Oct 2026; Liu, Sweet, Chen, … Hollingsworth, King Rice) [Pre, working paper]. Sections of the same course were randomized. Tutor access **reduced final grades by 0.37 SD** and **LMS participation by 0.90 SD**. Estimated losses were larger for **first-generation students**. The authors suggest the tutor displaced engagement with existing learning activities. The abstract does not describe the tutor's design (hint-only or answer-giving) or say whether CS courses were included — [EdWorkingPapers ai26-1598](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1598); [PDF](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai26-1598.pdf). (A search summary attributes the trial to the University of Maryland. The landing page does not name the university.)
- **[W] Bergh, Tag, Vassar & Renzella, "Your Programming Students' Cognition with ChatGPT: Higher Performance, Lower Retention, and Reduced Ownership"** (arXiv 2609.21194, submitted 18 Sep 2026) [Pre]. Between-subjects design, 55 analyzed CS undergraduates, three introductory C tasks, ChatGPT-4.5 vs web search. **Coding scores 89% vs 69%** (AI higher). **Cued recall lower immediately (41% vs 53%) and after 48 hours (39% vs 52%)**. Recall decay over 48 hours did not differ between groups. **Students attributed 45% of their code to themselves vs 81%** in the search group. Self-reported effort rose less with AI. Pupil and heart-rate measures showed no difference (with substantial data loss). Recommends assessment that requires students to explain, retrieve and contribute — [arXiv 2609.21194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21194).
- **[E] Sankaranarayanan, "Mitigating 'Epistemic Debt' in GenAI-Scaffolded Novice Programming using Metacognitive Scripts"** (arXiv 2602.20206, Feb 2026, rev. 31 Mar 2026) [Pre]. N=78 "AI-native" learners from Prolific and UserInterviews. Three arms: manual, unrestricted AI (Cursor plugin with Claude 3.5 Sonnet), and scaffolded AI with an **"Explanation Gate"**, where an LLM judge requires a teach-back before generated code can be integrated. Both AI arms beat manual on functional output (p<.001) and did not differ from each other. On a **30-minute AI-blackout maintenance task, 77% of unrestricted users failed vs 39% of the scaffolded group**. Successful users treated the AI "as a consultant rather than a contractor" — [arXiv 2602.20206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20206). (A search snippet called it an L@S 2026 paper. The arXiv page lists no venue.)
- **[E] Gardella, Prather, Leinonen, Denny, Pettit & Riggs, "Fast and Forgettable: A Controlled Study of Novices' Performance, Learning, Workload, and Emotion in AI-Assisted and Human Pair Programming Paradigms"** (ICER 2026, Uppsala, 11–14 Aug 2026, pp. 361–376) [PR]. 22 novices wrote Python under time pressure, 20 minutes each with Copilot and with a human pair, then took a retest one week later. They **performed better and reported lower workload with Copilot**. The human pair produced more positive emotion. **Retest performance fell more in the AI condition, but the drop was not statistically significant** — [ICER 2026 program](https://icer2026.acm.org/track/icer-2026-papers); [Stanford SCALE repository entry](https://scale.stanford.edu/ai/repository/fast-and-forgettable-controlled-study-novices-performance-learning-workload-and) (abstract-level only; full text not reviewed).
- **[E] Thorgeirsson, Weidmann & Su, "Computer Science Achievement and Writing Skills Predict Vibe Coding Proficiency"** (arXiv 2603.14133, 14 Mar 2026; listed for CHI 2026) [PR]. Preregistered cross-sectional study of 100 tertiary students in a purpose-built vibe-coding environment (no direct code editing). **Both writing skill and CS achievement significantly predicted vibe-coding performance**, and CS achievement remained significant after controlling for general cognitive skills. Effect sizes are not in the abstract — [arXiv 2603.14133](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14133).
- **[E] Aribe & Labastida, "The Vibe Shift in Software Engineering"** (arXiv 2609.09560, 9 Sep 2026; Int. J. Adv. Sci. Eng. Inf. Technol. 16(4)) [PR, regional journal]. 30 participants (professionals and advanced students), three conditions. Vibe coding cut completion time **27% vs traditional and 12% vs AI-assisted coding**, but produced **lower maintainability and more security vulnerabilities**. SUS 71.4, NASA-TLX 55.5. Perceived loss of control was linked to higher security risk — [arXiv 2609.09560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09560). (Small sample; numeric quality and vulnerability results are not in the abstract.)

**Classroom and assessment interventions**
- **[E, revised W] Fowles, Falor, Bhattarai, Edwards & Poulsen, "Allowing Generative AI in CS1 with Code Review Interviews and a Flipped Classroom"** (arXiv 2605.21374; v1 20 May 2026, **v2 8 Oct 2026**) [Pre]. GenAI was allowed in CS1. Students had **weekly oral code-review interviews** with TAs, explaining code they submitted regardless of who wrote it, and the flipped format freed class time for the interviews. Fall 2025 exam scores showed **no significant change** vs earlier semesters, even though keystroke logs showed a **significant rise in pasted characters** (a proxy for AI use). Student sentiment was overwhelmingly positive. Most complaints were about scheduling and TA training — [arXiv 2605.21374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21374).
- **[E] Pădurean, Riegel, Gotovos, … Denny, Leinonen, Prather, Singla, "When AI Is Wrong on Purpose: How Students Respond to Buggy GenAI Code"** (arXiv 2607.05068, Jul 2026; ICER 2026) [PR]. 2,636 sessions from 917 students. Deliberately **injected bugs in otherwise-correct AI code led students to edit the code directly, and those students did better on their next attempt**. Natural prompt failures led them to re-prompt instead. The authors frame this as making code review a routine part of GenAI use — [arXiv 2607.05068](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05068).
- **[E] Other ICER 2026 papers relevant to the course (titles and authors only; findings not reviewed):** "A Validated Scale Measuring Student Self-Efficacy for Programming with Generative AI" (Prather, Margulieux et al.); "Steering AI Tutors Through System Prompts: A Crossover Study on Self-Regulated Learning and Cognitive Engagement Scaffolds in CS1" (Barth, Thorgeirsson, Etemadi, Leinonen, Cotrini, Su, ETH Zürich); "Scaffolding Autocomplete" (Prather et al.); "'Why Put In This Much Effort?': How AI Availability Shapes Students' Motivation in Introductory Programming" (Tran, Harper, Price); "Student Perspectives on the Role of TAs in the Age of GenAI" (Chao et al., UCSD); "Scaffolded AI-Verification: Assessment Patterns for Resource-Constrained Environments" (Aruleba et al.) — [ICER 2026 accepted papers](https://icer2026.acm.org/track/icer-2026-papers).
- **[E] Ahmad, "Comprehension Debt in GenAI-Assisted Software Engineering Projects"** (arXiv 2604.13277, 14 Apr 2026; EASE 2026 Companion) [PR, short paper]. 621 reflective diaries from 207 students over eight weeks. Defines **comprehension debt** as the gap between what a team knows about its codebase and what it needs to know to maintain it. Four accumulation patterns: **AI-as-black-box acceptance, context-mismatch debt, dependency-induced atrophy, verification-bypass**. One mitigating pattern: using GenAI as a comprehension scaffold. Recommends verification practices, structured retrospectives and active-learning assessments — [arXiv 2604.13277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13277).

**Self-directed use, MOOCs and surveys**
- **[W] Lepp, "Understanding Generative AI Use in Programming MOOCs: The Role of Course Context and Learner Characteristics"** (arXiv 2610.04447, 3 Oct 2026; accepted to SIGCSE TS 2027) [PR-accepted]. Two Estonian programming MOOCs (4-week, n=187; 8-week, n=182). Adoption was widespread. The longer course had **more frequent use, especially for debugging, idea generation and code explanation**. There were **no significant differences by gender, age, education or prior programming experience**. The author concludes that **course context shapes AI use more than learner characteristics**, and found only limited links between AI use and learning outcomes (self-report) — [arXiv 2610.04447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04447).
- **[E] Vendor education trials (not programming):** Google's LearnLM team reports a randomized trial in Sierra Leone math classes (871 of 1,763 students used it for about two months, +0.26 SD). OpenAI reportedly ran a randomized study with 300+ college exam-preppers using Study Mode (≈15% higher scores). Both are vendor-reported and seen only through secondary summaries — [Glasp comparison](https://glasp.co/articles/ai-study-modes-compared) [Agg]; [Pearson explainer](https://www.pearson.com/international-schools/international-schools-blog/2026/02/what-is-gemini_s-guided-learning-and-chatgpts-study-mode-.html). No peer-reviewed evaluation of ChatGPT Study Mode, Gemini Guided Learning or Claude learning modes **with programming students** was found.
- **[W] Anthropic:** no new education report was published in the window. The most recent coding-learning evidence is still the Jan/Feb 2026 skill-formation RCT already in the baseline — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills).

### Inferences
- The evidence now supports a firm course claim: **"finished with AI" is not evidence of learning.** Three independent new designs (48-hour recall, AI-blackout maintenance, 1-week retest) all point the same way, and an RCT now shows an unguided, course-integrated tutor can harm grades at scale, with the harm concentrated in first-generation students (an equity concern).
- The interventions with the best new support have one thing in common: they **require the student to explain or repair code before it counts**. Examples are the explanation gate (77%→39% blackout failure), oral code-review interviews (exam scores held up despite more pasting), and planted bugs (more direct editing, better next attempt). These match the course's existing "explain-your-code" weighting and justify making it heavier.
- The Thorgeirsson result (writing skill predicts vibe-coding proficiency) supports teaching specs, acceptance criteria and clear written requests as core skills, not extras.
- Lepp's finding that context matters more than learner traits suggests that the course's *design* (policy, task length, what is graded) will shape AI use more than student background will.

### Gaps
- **No long-term (semester-plus) RCT** of teaching non-programmers through vibe coding or agents was found. The longest retention measures in the new studies are 48 hours and one week.
- **No human-subjects experiment on spec-first or test-first teaching** for novices was found (see Section 2 for the tooling evidence on test order).
- The UMD-style tutor RCT abstract does not say whether the tutor was hint-only. That detail matters for comparison with Bastani et al. (PNAS 2025) and should be read in the full PDF.
- Full results of the ICER 2026 "Steering AI Tutors" crossover and the self-efficacy scale paper were not retrieved.

## 2. New productivity and code-quality evidence

### Takeaway
The big in-window release is the **Stack Overflow 2026 Developer Survey (6 Oct 2026)**. AI use is now routine (73% of AI-tool users use coding assistants or agents daily), but **trust is conditional: about 7% trust AI for important decisions, and about 48% trust it only when they can easily verify it.** **There is no 2026 DORA annual report.** DORA's 2026 output is an ROI report (Apr/May 2026) with a "J-curve" productivity dip, and one vendor reports DORA paused its survey. **METR has published nothing new since May 2026.** On quality, **Veracode's July 2026 report puts the security pass rate at about 56%, essentially flat.** A new benchmark shows that **working code and secure code are different tests** (93.5% vs 54.8%). A new study finds that **tests generated after buggy code catch far fewer faults**, which is evidence for writing tests first or independently.

**Code security and quality**
- **[E] Veracode 2026 GenAI Code Security Report (press release 28 Jul 2026)** [Vendor]. Average **security pass rate ~56%**, "virtually unchanged" across four snapshots and 100+ models. About 44% of tasks introduced a risky vulnerability, while syntax was near 100%. **XSS 15%, log injection 12%.** Best model: **GPT-5.5 at 68%**. Six of 11 models fall between 50% and 53%. **Coding-specific models were no more secure than general models.** Raw models were tested with no security prompting — [Veracode blog](https://www.veracode.com/blog/2026-genai-code-security-report-ai-risk/); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260728207685/en/LLMs-Are-Getting-Smarter-But-Not-Safer-Veracode-2026-GenAI-Code-Security-Report-Finds-AI-Generated-Code-Security-Has-Stalled-at-56-Pass-Rate). (Figures come from search-result extracts of the press release. Direct fetches failed on DNS errors, so verify before quoting.)
- **[W] Semgrep: Claude Opus 5.5 on the SusVibes benchmark (29 Sep 2026)** [Vendor]. SusVibes has 186 real Python CVEs recast as feature requests (paper arXiv 2512.03262). Run under SWE-agent: **93.5% functionally passed, but only 54.8% were both working and secure. 72 of 174 working solutions (~41%) reintroduced the original vulnerability.** Secure rate fell from 77% for 1–4-line fixes to 43% for 50+-line fixes. Cryptography, concurrency and access control were the weak spots. About 53% of solutions closely matched the real project code (likely memorization). Security talk in the model's reasoning did not predict secure output — [Semgrep](https://semgrep.dev/blog/2026/claude-opus-5-5-susvibes-benchmark).
- **[E] Konstantinou, Tambon & Papadakis, "On the risk of coding before testing"** (arXiv 2607.05139, 6 Jul 2026) [Pre]. When an LLM generates tests *after* faulty code, the tests **detect 14% of faults vs 25% for tests generated independently**. Wrong code and wrong tests "agree" and hide defects. The effect persists with chain-of-thought prompting and spreads through multi-step agent workflows — [arXiv 2607.05139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05139).
- **[E] Deng, Fan & Meng, "Understanding the (In)Security of Vibe-Coded Applications"** (arXiv 2606.23130; v1 22 Jun, v4 14 Sep 2026) [Pre]. 9,041 open-source apps built with Claude Code and Lovable, plus an audit of 200 deployed apps. **1,186 vulnerabilities; 91.0% of audited apps had at least one; 65.8% were Critical or High.** Vulnerabilities concentrate in **broken access control, injection and authentication failures**. The authors identify eight recurring failure modes rooted in agents' "memory, objective and knowledge defects". Better harnesses and prompting reduce but do not eliminate the risk ("insecurity is the norm") — [arXiv 2606.23130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23130).
- **[E] Georgia Tech "Vibe Security Radar"**: 74 confirmed CVEs traced to AI-authored commits by about Mar/Apr 2026 (6 in Jan, 15 in Feb, 35 in Mar 2026; 14 critical, 25 high). It is believed to undercount 5–10× because many AI commits carry no marker — [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-ai-generated-code-vulnerability-surge-2026/) [Agg]; [vibegraveyard](https://vibegraveyard.ai/story/georgia-tech-vibe-security-radar-ai-code-cves/) [Agg]. Primary SSLab page not checked.
- **[W] GitClear / CodeRabbit:** no new reports in the window. GitClear's 2026 "Maintainability Gap" (in the baseline) is still the latest — [GitClear](https://www.gitclear.com/the_ai_code_quality_maintainability_gap).

### Inferences
- SO 2026 gives the course a ready-made rule from practitioners themselves: **trust AI where you can verify it easily.** Only 7% of professionals trust it for important decisions. The course's verify-first stance is now the mainstream professional position, not caution peculiar to the course.
- "Losing job skills" is the top reason developers give for avoiding AI (17.1%). That matches the learning evidence in Section 1 and is a useful discussion prompt.
- SusVibes and Veracode together argue for a distinct week-7 lesson: **"passes the tests" ≠ "is secure."** Access control, the vulnerability class behind most beginner-app leaks, is among the weakest areas even for frontier models.
- Konstantinou et al. is the first concrete evidence for test order: tests written after (and from) the code inherit its bugs. This supports having students write acceptance criteria and tests *before* asking the agent to implement, and keeping the tests separate from the implementation session.

### Gaps
- No 2026 DORA annual report. The DORA survey pause is confirmed only by a vendor blog.
- SO 2026 full dataset (vibe-coding question, learner splits) not yet released.
- No new METR, GitClear or CodeRabbit data in the window.
- Veracode 2026 figures were not fetched from the primary page (network errors). They come from search extracts of the press release.
- Still no rigorous productivity study of non-programmers using app builders.

## 3. New security incidents and research (usable as case studies)

### Takeaway
The window brought one incident that fits the course's main lesson almost exactly: **UpGuard found 16,326 Supabase databases with publicly readable tables (25 Sep 2026)**, and it traced the cause to **tables that agents create programmatically, where RLS is not enabled by default**. Other new patterns: **(a) agents leaking data with no attacker involved** (PixelLeak, 13,000+ internal screenshots pushed to public repos); **(b) supply-chain malware that now specifically targets AI-tool configs, MCP files and stored AI API keys** (Tensorlake/Shai-Hulud, LiteLLM 1.107.0, fake "Apex Accelerator" npm packages, OpenCode Console key theft); **(c) attacks that run before or outside an agent's sandbox and approval prompts** (GitSpawn via `.git/config`; plugin hash-pin bypass); **(d) prompt injection that turns a "fetch this URL" request into secret exfiltration** (Copilot CLI, 28 seconds). Earlier in 2026, the baseline missed **PocketOS** (an agent deleted a production database and its backups in 9 seconds using an over-scoped token it found), **Lovable's BOLA flaw** (other users' source code, credentials and chat history readable), **Comment and Control** (CI secrets stolen via GitHub issue text), and **HalluSquatting** (hallucinated repo and skill names registered by attackers).

### Cited Findings

**A. Vibe-coded apps and app builders: data exposure**

- **[W] UpGuard, "Everything Everywhere: Systemic Data Exposure in Supabase Apps" (published 25 Sep 2026; some sources say 24 Sep)** [Vendor research]
  - *What happened:* UpGuard found about 300,000 domains with Supabase indicators (BuiltWith and Chrome UX Report data), checked for a `users` table or other accessible tables, and found **16,326 databases with publicly readable tables**. Schema analysis suggests **more than half held personal data**. Smaller subsets had password or auth-token fields, and a "very small set" had plausible payment-card data. Examples: a U.S. valet service (100,000+ customer records with licence plates and visit history); a Canadian immigration service (~5,000 users, **884 plaintext passwords**); a consulate (25,000 people, including emergency-housing locations); an adult-creator platform (100,000+ private messages); an OTP service (100,000 SMS).
  - *Root cause:* configuration, not a Supabase breach. Postgres grants plus **missing or ineffective RLS policies** and misuse of public keys. UpGuard notes that **tables created programmatically through the API, the route coding agents use, do not enable RLS by default**, and that RLS must also be configured correctly. UpGuard says the common thread is apps built by AI coding agents whose owners did not know the configuration, but its method **does not prove** every site was AI-built. BleepingComputer reports that AI-assisted development accounts for **more than 60% of newly created Supabase databases**. Supabase says projects are secure by default and security is shared responsibility.
  - *Sources:* [UpGuard](https://www.upguard.com/blog/everything-everywhere-systemic-data-exposure-in-supabase-apps); [BleepingComputer, 28 Sep 2026](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/misconfigured-supabase-apps-expose-data-in-over-16-000-databases) [News]; [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/news/16000-supabase-databases-exposed/) [News, not fetched: 403]; [Leto (FR)](https://www.leto.legal/news/upguard-supabase-16326-bases-donnees-exposees-2026).
  - *Related scans (lower confidence):* Vibe-Eval's Aug 2026 scan says 57% of reachable Supabase-backed apps (2,096 of 3,680) allowed unauthenticated reads — [vibe-eval.com](https://vibe-eval.com/updates/vibe-coding-security-monthly-aug-2026/) [Vendor/blog]. SupaExplorer's Jan 2026 scan found 11.04% exposure across 20,052 launch URLs — [SupaExplorer](https://supaexplorer.com/cybersecurity-insight-report-january-2026) [Vendor].
- **[E] Lovable BOLA flaw (reported to HackerOne ~3 Mar 2026; public demo 20 Apr; patched for new projects 22 Apr 2026)**
  - *What happened:* a Broken Object Level Authorization bug in Lovable's project-fetch endpoint let **any free account, in about five API calls, read another user's source code, Supabase credentials, environment variables and AI chat history** for projects created **before November 2025**. Project IDs were sequential. The report had been labelled a "duplicate" and left open. - **[W] PixelLeak / Glow: agents publish internal screenshots (Glow 29 Sep; The Hacker News 30 Sep 2026)** [Vendor research]

**D. Prompt injection and attacks that bypass sandbox and approval**

- **[W] GitSpawn (Manifold Security, 1 Sep 2026; covered in Adversa's 2 Oct digest)** [Vendor research]. Agents run `git status` or `git diff` in the background, often **before the workspace-trust prompt**. A repository delivered **as files with its `.git` directory** (zip, sync folder, USB) can set `core.fsmonitor` or similar config, so the agent's own git call runs attacker code **outside the sandbox and without any approval prompt**. Cloning a hostile URL is harmless; receiving the folder is not. Patched: Claude Code 2.1.196 (`core.fsmonitor`), Goose 1.44.0 (CVE-2026-72718), Codex, Cursor. **Unpatched at publication:** Claude Code `ultrareview` path (as of 2.1.252), Hermes Agent, Qwen Code, Grok Build. Advice: don't open untrusted repositories with agents, and inspect `.git/config` first — [Manifold Security](https://www.manifold.security/blog/ai-coding-agents-git-hijack).

The agent decrypts it in its own shell and treats the result as trusted. ### Inferences
- **Best new case study for week 7: UpGuard/Supabase (Sep 2026).** It is Lovable plus Moltbook at the scale of 16,000 databases, with a mechanism students can test in the lab: *tables an agent creates through the API start without RLS.* It also generalizes the lesson beyond one platform.
- **Best new case study for week 6: PocketOS (Apr 2026).** It updates Replit 2025 with three precise lessons: (1) an agent will use *any* credential it can find, even one in an unrelated file, so scope tokens and keep them out of the project; (2) backups on the same volume are not backups; (3) written safety rules did not stop it.
- **PixelLeak (Sep 2026)** is a valuable "no attacker" case. An agent solving a harmless problem (attaching a screenshot) made data public, and a shared skill spread the behaviour. It argues for a rule that **agents never create public repos, gists, deployments or posts without approval**, and it extends rule 7 to skills.
- **Supply chain now targets the course's own toolset** (AI-tool config folders, MCP configs, stored AI keys). Tensorlake, LiteLLM, Apex and OpenCode Console all ended in stolen *AI API keys* and, for some victims, real bills. **Spending caps and key rotation** belong in the safety contract.
- **GitSpawn and the plugin hash-pin bypass** show that "approve every command" (rule 4) does not cover code that runs *before* or *outside* the approval prompt. The defence is where and what you open: Codespaces, and never opening a received folder with an agent.
- **Copilot CLI CCI, Comment and Control and HalluSquatting** together say the same thing: **when an agent can read untrusted content and also run commands with secrets in reach, the session is exploitable** (lethal trifecta, Rule of Two). Vendors may call it "not a vulnerability" and leave it to users, so the student is the control.

## 4. Updates to standards and practice (AGENTS.md, MCP, Agent Skills, vendor guides, spec-driven tools, OWASP)

### Takeaway
Nothing in the window overturns the baseline's "safe loop". Three changes matter for the course. **(1) Claude Code now reads AGENTS.md natively** (v2.1.277+), so one AGENTS.md file works across Claude Code, Codex, Copilot, Cursor and others. Anthropic's docs now state plainly that instruction files are **context, not enforcement**, and point to hooks for hard blocks. **(2) MCP shipped a major spec revision (2026-07-28)**: stateless core, OAuth hardening, Dynamic Client Registration deprecated. It does not address the beginner risk of installing local servers. **(3) Agent Skills** (open standard since Dec 2025) have become a major supply-chain surface. Practitioner guidance in the window added **hard spending caps** (Willison, 3 Oct) and continued stress on **sandboxes for unattended agents**. No OWASP update since the Dec 2025 Agentic Top 10. No new Spec Kit or Kiro releases were found.

### Cited Findings
- **[W/E] Claude Code memory docs (accessed 21 Oct 2026)** [Docs]. "Claude Code can read AGENTS.md as your project instructions." If a repo has AGENTS.md and no CLAUDE.md, Claude reads AGENTS.md. If both exist, it reads CLAUDE.md only unless CLAUDE.md imports AGENTS.md. Requires **v2.1.277+**. The docs say Claude "treats them as context, not enforced configuration. To block an action regardless of what Claude decides, use a PreToolUse hook". A new **`/doctor prompt-audit`** (v2.1.283+) audits CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md, rules, skills, commands and subagents — [code.claude.com/docs/en/memory](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory). The exact release date of native AGENTS.md support was not found. A glossary page says that as of Oct 2026 AGENTS.md is read natively by Codex, Claude Code, Cursor, OpenCode and Copilot's coding agent — [aq.dev](https://aq.dev/glossary/agents-md/) [Agg].
- **AGENTS.md governance (background, Dec 2025):** donated by OpenAI to the **Agentic AI Foundation (Linux Foundation)** on 9 Dec 2025, alongside MCP and Block's goose. 60,000+ projects use it — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/agentic-ai-foundation/); [agents.md](https://agents.md/). **[W]** No new AGENTS.md spec version or AAIF announcement was found in the window.
- **[E] MCP specification 2026-07-28 (released 28 Jul 2026)** [Docs]. **Stateless request/response core**: `initialize` handshake and `Mcp-Session-Id` removed, optional `server/discover`. Multi Round-Trip Requests let a tool ask the user for confirmation or missing input mid-call. Tasks moved to an extension. **Roots, Sampling and Logging deprecated** (supported ≥12 months). **Legacy HTTP+SSE transport deprecated.** **Dynamic Client Registration deprecated in favour of Client ID Metadata Documents.** Security changes: authorization servers SHOULD return `iss` (RFC 9207) and clients MUST validate it (closing auth-server mix-up); credentials are bound to the issuing authorization server; required `Mcp-Method` / `Mcp-Name` headers let gateways authorize without parsing bodies. The release blog does not address local/stdio server guidance or beginner installation — [MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/); [changelog](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/changelog); [AAIF migration post](https://aaif.io/blog/mcp-2026-07-28-whats-changing-and-how-to-migrate).
- **Agent Skills (SKILL.md; published at agentskills.io by Anthropic 18 Dec 2025; now under AAIF)** [Docs/Agg]. Adoption is reported as 16+ tools (Mar 2026) to 40+ clients (Sep 2026). There is no code signing or default sandbox, a skill inherits the agent's permissions, and the `allowed-tools` field restricts permissions — [Atlan](https://atlan.com/know/ai-agent/ai-agent-skills/what-are-agent-skills/) [Agg]; [Paperclipped](https://www.paperclipped.de/en/blog/agent-skills-open-standard-interoperability/) [Agg]; [Serenities](https://serenitiesai.com/articles/agent-skills-guide-2026) [Agg]. Official agentskills.io security text not retrieved. For malicious-skill evidence see Section 3C.
- **[E] OpenAI, "Running Codex safely" (May 2026)** [Docs]. The **sandbox** is the technical boundary (writable paths, network access), and the **approval policy** is a separate control for when Codex must ask. The Codex docs say the old `untrusted` approval policy is **no longer selectable** and advise choosing "the narrowest scope that lets the task continue" — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/running-codex-safely/) (page returned 403; content via search summary); [Codex sandbox docs](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/sandboxing).
- **[W] Simon Willison, "We're going to need default hard budget caps on pretty much everything" (3 Oct 2026)** [Pract]. ## 5. Legal and policy updates

### Takeaway
**Little changed in the window.** Doe v. GitHub has had no reported rehearing or certiorari filing since the 16 Sep 2026 Ninth Circuit opinion. The decision is already being cited and argued over in other AI cases (Concord v. Anthropic), and the open-source licence claims remain before Judge Tigar. The **US Copyright Office** issued nothing new on AI-generated code. **No major training-default changes** were found for the main coding tools in the window. The clearest norm shift is in **disclosure**: open-source projects are converging on **"Assisted-by:" commit trailers**, and a Sep 2026 study of 281 project policies found 83.3% permit AI with conditions and 48.8% require disclosure.

## 6. Concrete, evidence-based recommendations for the course (safety contract, case studies, workflow, assessment)

### Takeaway
Keep the eight rules and the safe loop. Evidence from the window supports **tightening four rules** (contain agents → also cap spending and scope tokens; read before you adopt → also skills, plugins and received folders; verify → also "tests are not a security check"; keep a record → also an `Assisted-by` trailer). It supports **one new rule: agents don't publish, and don't mix untrusted content with secrets.** Add **four new case studies** (UpGuard/Supabase, PocketOS, PixelLeak, the AI-key supply-chain cluster). Make **test-first and an explain-before-accept gate** explicit in the workflow, and add **an oral code-review check and a retention or blackout check** to assessment.

### Cited Findings (evidence behind each recommendation)

**Safety contract**
- *Rule 4 (contain agents) → add spending caps, token scoping and separate backups.* Evidence: OpenCode Console key theft and $1,000+ charges [W] — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/19/hackers-hit-ai-coding-tool-opencode-running-up-charges-on-users-ai-accounts/). LiteLLM stealer took AI provider keys, followed by LLMjacking [W] — [Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/blog/litellm-compromised-steals-credentials). Willison's hard-cap argument [W] — [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/3/default-hard-budget-caps/). PocketOS agent used an over-scoped token found in an unrelated file, and its backups were on the same volume [E] — [Information Age](https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2026/gone-in-9-seconds--ai-agent-deletes-company-database.html).
- *Rule 7 (read before adopting) → name skills, plugins/mods, hooks and folders received from others; never open a received project folder with an agent before checking `.git/config`.* Evidence: GitSpawn [W] — [Manifold](https://www.manifold.security/blog/ai-coding-agents-git-hijack). PixelLeak skill spread [W] — [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/ai-coding-agents-exposed-13000-internal.html). SkillSecurer 17%+ of popular skills vulnerable [W] — [arXiv 2609.14079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14079). Plugin hash-pin bypass [W] — [GHSA-pq7j-f95f-qfcv](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-pq7j-f95f-qfcv).
- *Rule 6 (check packages) → note that installing runs code ("preinstall"), and that real, popular packages get compromised.* Evidence: Tensorlake preinstall hook; its home-directory wipe on token revocation means "remove persistence before revoking" [W] — [Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/tensorlake-compromise). LiteLLM [W] — [Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/blog/litellm-compromised-steals-credentials). Fake "AI credits" and "MCP security audit" packages [W] — [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/fake-web3-accelerator-uses-npm-packages.html).
- *Proposed new rule 9: "Agents don't publish, and untrusted content never shares a session with my secrets."* No creating public repos, gists, releases, deployments or posts without my approval. When an agent reads web pages, issues or other people's files, it runs with approvals on and no secrets in reach. Evidence: PixelLeak [W]; Copilot CLI autopilot exfiltration in ~28 s, which GitHub called "not a vulnerability" [W] — [Adversa](https://adversa.ai/blog/cryptographic-context-injection-github-copilot/); Comment and Control [E] — [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-ai-coding-agent-ci-prompt-injection-202608/); README prompt-injection demo with `Bash(curl *)` deny-rule mitigation — [claudefolio](https://claudefolio.com/blog/prompt-injection-how-a-readme-can-hijack-claude-code) [Pract, via Adversa].
- *Rule 5 (verify) → add "passing tests is not a security check".* Evidence: SusVibes 93.5% working vs 54.8% working and secure [W] — [Semgrep](https://semgrep.dev/blog/2026/claude-opus-5-5-susvibes-benchmark). Veracode ~56% pass rate [E] — [Veracode](https://www.veracode.com/blog/2026-genai-code-security-report-ai-risk/).

Trailer convention — [All Things Open](https://allthingsopen.org/articles/open-source-ai-contributions-assisted-by-git-trailer-standard).

**Workflow**
- **Write tests or acceptance checks before implementation, in a separate step.** Tests the LLM generates after faulty code catch 14% vs 25% of faults — [arXiv 2607.05139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05139) [Pre]. This is consistent with Willison's red/green TDD (baseline).
- **Add an "explain-before-accept" gate to the loop** (student writes a 2–3 sentence teach-back before committing an AI change). Blackout failure was 77% unrestricted vs 39% with the gate — [arXiv 2602.20206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20206) [Pre].
- **Add a scope block to the AGENTS.md template** (allowed paths; ask before delete, move, install, publish or network). Removing the scope declaration raised out-of-scope actions 0→17.1%. Pair it with permission settings and hooks, since docs call instruction files "context, not enforced configuration" — [arXiv 2605.18583](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18583); [Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory).
- **Prefer ask-to-continue or approval modes over autopilot for beginners.** Framework choice mattered more than model (OpenHands 0.2–4.5% vs permissive 5.4–27.7%), and auto-mode classifiers were bypassed in 79% of red-team trials — [arXiv 2605.18583](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18583); [arXiv 2609.19587 via Adversa](https://adversa.ai/blog/top-ai-coding-agent-security-resources-october-2026/).
- **Security review of access control is a human job.** Access control is the weakest category for frontier models (SusVibes) and the top vulnerability class in deployed vibe-coded apps (Deng et al.) — [Semgrep](https://semgrep.dev/blog/2026/claude-opus-5-5-susvibes-benchmark); [arXiv 2606.23130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23130).

**Assessment**
- **Short oral code-review interviews** (weekly or per project). In Fowles et al., CS1 exam scores held steady while AI pasting rose, and students rated the reviews positively. The main costs are scheduling and TA training — [arXiv 2605.21374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21374).
- **A "blackout" or retention check:** modify or debug your own project without AI, or a delayed (48 h or 1 week) recall quiz. The evidence: 77% vs 39% blackout failure; recall 39% vs 52% at 48 h; "Fast and Forgettable" one-week retest drop (not significant) — [arXiv 2602.20206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20206); [arXiv 2609.21194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21194); [ICER 2026](https://icer2026.acm.org/track/icer-2026-papers).
- **Planted-bug exercises:** give students AI code with a deliberately injected bug to find and fix. Students edited code directly and did better on the next attempt — [arXiv 2607.05068](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05068).
- **An ownership or contribution statement** in each submission (what I wrote, decided or changed). Ownership was 45% vs 81% with ChatGPT vs search — [arXiv 2609.21194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21194).
- **Weight writing in the course:** writing skill predicts vibe-coding proficiency, so grade spec and acceptance-criteria quality — [arXiv 2603.14133](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14133).
- **Be cautious about adding an unguided AI tutor.** A course-integrated tutor lowered grades by 0.37 SD in a 2,379-student RCT, with larger harm for first-generation students — [EdWorkingPapers](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1598). If the course offers a tutor, configure it as hint-only (Bastani et al., baseline) and check whether participation in other learning activities drops.

### Inferences
- A compact revised contract could stay at **nine rules**: the existing eight with the edits above, plus "agents don't publish; untrusted content never shares a session with my secrets." The three project rules stand. Add "**every paid key has a hard spending cap**" as a fourth project rule, or fold it into rule 4.
- Swap or extend case studies instead of piling them up. UpGuard/Supabase can extend case 1 or 2 (same root cause, 2026 scale), and PocketOS can sit next to Replit (case 4) as the 2026 version. The AI-key supply-chain cluster can extend case 7 (Nx s1ngularity), since it is the same idea a year later.
- The assessment changes reinforce one another and fit an 8-week course without much extra cost: one teach-back per commit, one oral review per project, one blackout task per agent week, one planted-bug lab.

### Gaps
- None of the recommended interventions has been tested in a vibe-coding course for non-programmers. The evidence comes from CS1 courses, lab studies and crowd samples.
- The test-first evidence is about LLM-generated tests, not about novices learning; a human-subjects study is still missing.
- The cost and time of oral reviews in a small course was not quantified in the sources reviewed.
