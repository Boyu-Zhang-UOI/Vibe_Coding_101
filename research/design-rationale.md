# Why the Course Looks Like This

> This page explains the main design decisions in Vibe Coding 101 and the evidence behind each one. It's written for instructors adapting the course, for reviewers, and for students who want to know why they're being asked to do things this way.
> The evidence comes from the course's research review, completed in September 2026 ([landscape report](landscape-report-2026-09.md)), its October 2026 update ([update report](landscape-update-2026-10.md)), and the detailed [research notes](notes/). The [October 2026 update](#october-2026-update) section below maps each change in the October revision to its evidence.

## The short version

**Teach the loop, not the tool.** University and industry courses on AI-assisted programming have converged on one shape: vibe-code and deploy something in week one, climb from chat to editor to agent, add specs, git, tests, review and security along the way, and grade students on explaining their code rather than on whether it runs ([report](landscape-report-2026-09.md)). The research supports that shape. AI help raises task completion, but without structure it weakens debugging and code comprehension, and struggling students come away believing they understand more than they do ([Prather et al.](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3632620.3671116); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills); [Bastani et al.](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)). Vibe-coded apps fail in predictable places: public database keys without access rules, leaked secrets, invented package names and destructive agent commands ([report](landscape-report-2026-09.md#beginner-apps-break-where-browser-meets-server-not-in-the-syntax)). And the free tools change monthly.

So the course teaches one tool-agnostic workflow, [the Safe Loop](../resources/safe-loop.md); gives every tool two tested fallbacks; saves everything to GitHub; checks understanding hardest in the agent weeks; builds its security unit around real breaches; and keeps every product detail in one dated file, [TOOLS.md](../TOOLS.md).

**October 2026 adds friction on purpose.** New controlled studies agree that AI-assisted novices finish more and remember less, and that what helps is making students explain or repair code before it counts ([update report](landscape-update-2026-10.md#four-new-studies-agree-ai-assisted-novices-finish-more-and-remember-less)). So the Safe Loop gained two habits, *check first* and *teach back*; the safety contract gained a ninth rule; and the course became a 3-credit course with two studios a week, giving the extra practice room to happen ([details](#october-2026-update)).

## How strong is the evidence?

Not all of it is equally strong, and the course is designed with that in mind.

- **Randomized trials** exist for a few questions: how AI help affects learning ([Anthropic, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills); [Bastani et al.](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122), which studied high-school *math*, not coding; and a 2,379-student trial in which a course-integrated AI tutor lowered final grades, [EdWorkingPapers 26-1598](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1598), whose abstract doesn't say what the tutor did or whether the course was in computing) and how it affects experienced developers' speed ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)).
- **Small controlled studies** from 2026 measure what happens after the task: recall two days later and claimed ownership ([Bergh et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21194), 55 students), and maintenance with the AI switched off ([Sankaranarayanan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20206), 78 learners). Both are preprints.
- **Observational studies and surveys** describe what novices actually do when vibe coding ([Geng et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.22614); [Fawzy et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24521v1)) and how courses are assessed ([Geng et al. syllabus study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05898); [ACM Task Force](https://acm-education-genai-task-force.github.io/ACM_Taskforce_GenAI_Report_16Feb26.pdf)).
- **Course reports** such as CMU 15-113's are rich but self-reported, and its instructor notes that an LLM drafted most of the report before he revised it ([Student Wisdom report](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~113/bestPractices.html)). The Fall 2026 report was generated by an AI from about 630 anonymized answers with only light manual review; its quotes and statistics were checked by script, but its theme counts are an AI's reading ([CMU F26 report](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~113/bestPracticesF26.html)).
- **Practitioner consensus** (vendor guides and well-known engineers) supports the workflow itself. It is strong agreement, not controlled evidence.
- **Security figures** come mostly from vendors with a commercial interest ([Veracode](https://www.veracode.com/blog/spring-2026-genai-code-security/); [GitGuardian](https://blog.gitguardian.com/the-state-of-secrets-sprawl-2026/); [UpGuard](https://www.upguard.com/blog/everything-everywhere-systemic-data-exposure-in-supabase-apps); [Semgrep](https://semgrep.dev/blog/2026/claude-opus-5-5-susvibes-benchmark)), though they point the same way, and one academic audit agrees ([Deng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23130)).

Where a decision rests on weaker evidence, the tables below say so.

## Decisions and evidence

### Sequence and pacing

| Decision | Evidence | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| **Start with vibe coding in week 1**, and deploy something that day | Across university and industry courses, students vibe-code and deploy a prototype in week one ([report](landscape-report-2026-09.md)). DeepLearning.AI's zero-experience course follows the same describe → generate → customize loop ([Build with Andrew](https://www.deeplearning.ai/courses/build-with-andrew)). The course teaches Karpathy's original meaning *and* why the field moved on ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Mar/19/vibe-coding/); [Karpathy, 2026](https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/sequoia-ascent-2026/)), because students will meet the term used both ways ([Kim & Yegge](https://itrevolution.com/product/vibe-coding-book/)). | [Week 1](../weeks/01-hello-vibe-coding/) · [Syllabus §1](../SYLLABUS.md#1-course-description) |
| **A ladder of tools**: chat with preview → app builders → editor assistant → own server → agent | CMU 15-113 put its first project on browser chatbots so students would feel that workflow's limits before meeting an editor assistant ([Student Wisdom report](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~113/bestPractices.html)). Northeastern's CS 7180 climbs the same ladder explicitly ([NEU CS 7180](https://johnguerra.co/classes/aiCoding_spring_2026/)). Each rung comes after the habits it needs. | [Syllabus §4](../SYLLABUS.md#the-ladder-of-tools) |
| **Spec first, in week 3**, with WHEN … THE APP SHALL … criteria | Vendor guides agree on spec or plan before code ([GitHub Spec Kit](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/generative-ai/spec-driven-development-with-ai-get-started-with-a-new-open-source-toolkit/); [Codex best practices](https://learn.chatgpt.com/guides/best-practices); [Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). EARS-style criteria are learnable by non-programmers ([Kiro](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/feature-specs/)). CMU students rated writing the spec as *harder* than managing agents ([Student Wisdom report](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~113/bestPractices.html)), so it is practiced early, before agents; in Fall 2026 CMU itself moved spec-driven development from homework 8 to week 5 ([CMU F26 report](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~113/bestPracticesF26.html)). Writing skill predicts vibe-coding proficiency ([Thorgeirsson et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14133)), and every spec now gets a Goldfish test ([EGM](https://github.com/eecs498-aase/materials/blob/HEAD/egm/README.md)). *No study yet shows that spec-first helps novices learn* ([update report](landscape-update-2026-10.md#professional-developers-trust-ai-only-where-they-can-verify-it)). | [Week 3](../weeks/03-spec-first/) · [SPEC template](../templates/SPEC.md) · [Goldfish test](../resources/prompt-patterns.md#18-the-goldfish-test) |
| **Read, debug and own the code in week 4**, with an AI-off Debug Clinic and "the AI plans, you write, the AI reviews" | Debugging is the skill AI use erodes most ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills)). CMU's highest understanding scores came on the assignment where students read and partly wrote the code ([Student Wisdom report](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~113/bestPractices.html)). CS1-LLM's final exam was mostly AI-free tracing, explaining, testing and debugging, and results largely matched pre-AI cohorts ([CS1-LLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.15379)). Coding practice, not awareness, predicted who checked AI code ([Fawzy et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24521v1)). Since October 2026, week 4 also has students write their first automated test, from a worked example, so that tests can be written *before* the code from then on: tests an AI writes after faulty code catch fewer faults ([Konstantinou et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05139)). | [Week 4](../weeks/04-read-debug-own-it/) · [Debug Clinic](../weeks/04-read-debug-own-it/debug-clinic/) · [check first](../resources/safe-loop.md#check-first) |
| **Deploy early and often**: GitHub Pages in week 1, a dedicated deployment and secrets lab in week 5 | CMU students reported that deployment took more time than coding ([Student Wisdom report](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~113/bestPractices.html)). Deploying a small thing first makes the week 5 full-stack deploy a second attempt, not a first. | [Week 1](../weeks/01-hello-vibe-coding/) · [Week 5](../weeks/05-apis-secrets-servers/) |
| **Hold agents until week 6** | UChicago and Northeastern both reserve autonomous agents for the second half ([UChicago](https://mpcs-courses.cs.uchicago.edu/2025-26/spring/courses/mpcs-51238-1); [NEU](https://johnguerra.co/classes/aiCoding_spring_2026/)). DORA finds AI amplifies existing practice, and names strong version control and small batches as conditions for it to help ([DORA](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/2025_dora_ai_capabilities_model.pdf)), so git, specs and tests come first. At CMU, understanding and code-reading scores fell once agents took over ([Student Wisdom report](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~113/bestPractices.html)). | [Week 6](../weeks/06-agents/) |
| **Open the agent week by "being the agent"**: change an app using only an agent's tools | Utah had students change a calculator using only an agent's tools ([Utah calculator repo](https://github.com/utah-cs3960-sp26/calculator)). Stanford's Fall 2026 course opens by showing students how an agent works inside ([CS146S](https://themodernsoftware.dev/)). Both show the loop at no cost before students hand it control. | [Be the Agent](../weeks/06-agents/be-the-agent/) |
| **Security and review in week 7**, built around an attack-your-own-database lab | The most instructive failures share one root cause: a public key plus missing authorization rules ([Superblocks on Lovable](https://www.superblocks.com/blog/lovable-vulnerabilities); [Wiz on Moltbook](https://www.wiz.io/blog/exposed-moltbook-database-reveals-millions-of-api-keys)). In September 2026 the same root cause turned up in 16,326 Supabase databases, and tables that agents create through the API start without RLS ([UpGuard](https://www.upguard.com/blog/everything-everywhere-systemic-data-exposure-in-supabase-apps)). Newer models have not written more secure code ([Veracode Spring 2026](https://www.veracode.com/blog/spring-2026-genai-code-security/)); working code was also secure only 54.8% of the time in one 2026 benchmark ([Semgrep](https://semgrep.dev/blog/2026/claude-opus-5-5-susvibes-benchmark)), so waiting for better models is not a plan. The attack page is now hosted on the course site, so the lab needs no extra repository (review H7). | [Week 7](../weeks/07-security-and-review/) · [RLS Attack Lab](../weeks/07-security-and-review/rls-lab/) · [case studies](../resources/case-studies.md) |
| **Four projects, from toy to real stakes**: home page → game → a tool you'd use → deployed capstone | The near-universal opening is a single-file page, then a game, which needs no backend and gives instant feedback ([DLAI](https://www.deeplearning.ai/courses/build-with-andrew); [Google codelab](https://codelabs.developers.google.com/vibe-code-with-gemini-in-aistudio)). Vanderbilt builds on learners' own tools and files ([Coursera](https://www.coursera.org/learn/build-anything-with-ai)). The capstone follows CMU 15-113's deployed app with live URL, README, prompt log and demo video ([CMU 15-113](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~113/)). | [Projects](../projects/) |

### Tools and cost

| Decision | Evidence | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| **$0 required** | The free-tool landscape can support an eight-week course ([report](landscape-report-2026-09.md#free-tiers-still-exist-in-2026-but-they-are-capped-age-restricted-and-change-monthly)). Michigan runs on free local models and CMU on free tools ([UMich](https://eecs498-aase.github.io/syllabus.html); [CMU 15-113](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~113/)). Where a university plan ran short, UChicago students had to buy a paid plan ([UChicago](https://mpcs-courses.cs.uchicago.edu/2025-26/spring/courses/mpcs-51238-1)), and MOOC learners object to subscription costs ([Coursera reviews](https://www.coursera.org/learn/claude-code/reviews)). With an AI tutor harming first-generation students most in a large trial ([EdWorkingPapers](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1598)), a free-tools course is an equity choice, provided it adds friction for everyone rather than more AI help. | [TOOLS.md](../TOOLS.md) · [Syllabus §10](../SYLLABUS.md#10-tools-and-cost) |
| **A primary tool and two fallbacks per layer; switching is a planned drill** | In CMU 15-113, free credits on several tools ran out mid-assignment for at least one student ([Student Wisdom report](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~113/bestPractices.html)). | **Two-layer materials**: a concept layer that names no versions or limits, plus one dated tool handout | Nearly every tool a 2025 syllabus would have used changed its free tier, price, name or owner within fifteen months, and several disappeared ([report](landscape-report-2026-09.md#free-tiers-still-exist-in-2026-but-they-are-capped-age-restricted-and-change-monthly)). Recorded screen demos go out of date within months ([VibeGym](https://vibegym.space/blog/best-vibe-coding-courses-for-beginners)); Maven revises its curriculum every quarter ([Maven](https://maven.com/kentro/context-engineering-for-coding)). | [CONTRIBUTING: two-layer rule](../CONTRIBUTING.md#the-two-layer-rule) · [TOOLS.md](../TOOLS.md) |
| **Copilot in VS Code, in a Codespace, as the backbone** from week 4 | It is the most dependable no-cost agent path: agent mode, MCP and a CLI on the free plan, all three operating systems, and no 18+ restriction ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/get-started/plans)); re-checked unchanged on 21 October 2026 ([update report](landscape-update-2026-10.md#copilot-still-anchors-a-0-course-while-four-free-tiers-shrank)). A remote environment such as Codespaces is a recommended sandbox for agents ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Sep/30/designing-agentic-loops/)). | [setup/codespaces.md](../setup/codespaces.md) · [Week 4](../weeks/04-read-debug-own-it/) · [Week 6](../weeks/06-agents/) |
| **Switch LLM providers by configuration**, not code | Major free providers accept OpenAI's request format, so an app that reads the base URL from an environment variable can switch by changing one setting ([report](landscape-report-2026-09.md#free-tiers-still-exist-in-2026-but-they-are-capped-age-restricted-and-change-monthly)). | [Week 5](../weeks/05-apis-secrets-servers/) |
| **GitHub is the save point** for everything, whatever tool made it | Builders disappear or expire: GitHub Spark and Firebase Studio were shut down, and Replit's free published apps expire, taking work with them ([report](landscape-report-2026-09.md#give-every-layer-a-primary-tool-and-two-fallbacks)). | **Variant stacks for under-18 and EEA/UK/Swiss cohorts** | Several tools are 18+ only, and the Gemini API's free tier may not serve users in the EEA, UK or Switzerland ([report](landscape-report-2026-09.md#free-tiers-still-exist-in-2026-but-they-are-capped-age-restricted-and-change-monthly)). Settle this before enrollment, not in week one. | [instructor/variants.md](../instructor/variants.md) |

### Assessment

| Decision | Evidence | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| **Oral walkthroughs in weeks 4, 6 and 8**, clustered in the agent weeks | CMU 15-113 follows each project with a mock-job-interview check; UCSD can hold a grade until a student walks staff through the code; CS1-LLM required a video explaining one function ([CMU 15-113](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~113/); [UCSD](https://ucsd-cse-115-215.github.io/sp26/index.html); [CS1-LLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.15379)). 68% of surveyed educators had already changed their assessment, oral defenses among the changes ([ACM Task Force](https://acm-education-genai-task-force.github.io/ACM_Taskforce_GenAI_Report_16Feb26.pdf)). | **Publish six skills students must show without AI**, and require each to be shown once | 31.1% of CS1-LLM students were not confident which problems they should be able to solve without the AI, and the instructors said they should have spelled it out ([CS1-LLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.15379)). The October review found the skills weren't really gated: exit tickets are completion-graded and walkthrough scores are compensatory (review A-09). Since October 2026 each skill must be shown at least once in a walkthrough, retake or follow-up, and all six are needed for a C or higher. | [resources/without-ai-skills.md](../resources/without-ai-skills.md) · [walkthroughs](../assessment/oral-walkthroughs.md#the-six-skills-you-must-show-without-ai) |
| **AI-free reflections and peer reviews**, submitted on Canvas | CMU 17-316 bans AI from reflections, which must describe concrete moments when the tool surprised the student ([CMU 17-316 HW1](https://ai-developer-tools.github.io/assignments/HW1/)); UCSD bans AI from peer reviews ([UCSD](https://ucsd-cse-115-215.github.io/sp26/index.html)). Learners misjudge their own learning when AI helps ([Prather et al.](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3632620.3671116); [Bastani et al.](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)); writing without it is where that shows. Since October 2026 this graded personal writing goes on Canvas, not in public repositories (University of Idaho FSH 4700 F-6 and FSH 2600; review A-02–A-04). | [REFLECTION template](../templates/REFLECTION.md) · [CODE_REVIEW template](../templates/CODE_REVIEW.md) · [rubric](../assessment/rubrics.md#reflections-and-peer-reviews) |
| **Labs graded on effort, with cheap process logs** (`PROMPTS.md`) | CMU 15-113 grades homework on effort; Utah grades in-class work on effort and fails assignments with missing AI logs; Michigan requires an AI log ([CMU 15-113](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~113/); [Utah](https://github.com/utah-cs3960-sp26/syllabus/blob/main/syllabus.md); [UMich](https://eecs498-aase.github.io/syllabus.html)). Pasting share links into a file keeps logs cheap ([NJIT](https://kelloggm.github.io/martinjkellogg.com/teaching/cs485-sp26/)). Effort grading plus oral checks avoids an arms race over detecting AI use. | [rubric](../assessment/rubrics.md#weekly-labs-and-homework) · [PROMPTS template](../templates/PROMPTS.md) |
| **Capstone graded on spec, tests, security and deployment**; a working demo is required but not sufficient | AI makes brittle prototypes easy, so Utah gives credit only for edge cases, how features interact, and taste ([Utah](https://github.com/utah-cs3960-sp26/syllabus/blob/main/syllabus.md)); Michigan's rubric is half spec and design, half implementation and tests ([UMich](https://eecs498-aase.github.io/syllabus.html)). | [capstone rubric](../assessment/rubrics.md#capstone) · [capstone brief](../projects/capstone.md) |
| **Participation counts (15%)**, across 16 studios | In the syllabus study, all conventionally graded courses counted participation, at a median weight of 17.5% ([Geng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05898)). | [rubric](../assessment/rubrics.md#in-class-work-and-participation) |

### Teaching format

| Decision | Evidence | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| **Concept talks of 20 minutes or less; most studio time is lab** | Learner reviews reward building and punish lecture-heavy courses ([Coursera reviews](https://www.coursera.org/learn/claude-code/reviews)). | Every week's `slides.md`, e.g. [week 1](../weeks/01-hello-vibe-coding/slides.md) |
| **Core and stretch homework tiers**, with a "Minimum to keep up" box | Maven splits homework this way ([Maven](https://maven.com/kentro/context-engineering-for-coding)). Weaker students benefit least from AI ([ACM Task Force](https://acm-education-genai-task-force.github.io/ACM_Taskforce_GenAI_Report_16Feb26.pdf); [Prather et al.](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3632620.3671116)), so the core tier stays achievable and support concentrates there. Stated times must add up: CMU re-estimated its API and backend homeworks from 2–3 hours to about six ([CMU F26 report](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~113/bestPracticesF26.html)), and the October review found several weeks over budget on paper (review A, F07, F14). | Every week's `homework.md`, e.g. [week 1](../weeks/01-hello-vibe-coding/homework.md) |
| **One workflow, the Safe Loop, in every lab from week 2**, plus the two-strikes rule, *check first* and *teach back* | Vendor guides and practitioners describe nearly the same loop: plan, small task, verify with evidence, read the diff, commit ([Claude Code](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices); [Codex](https://learn.chatgpt.com/guides/best-practices); [Osmani](https://addyosmani.com/blog/ai-coding-workflow/); [Willison](https://simonwillison.net/guides/agentic-engineering-patterns/)). Observed professionals plan first and check all agent output ([Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14012)). After two failed corrections, start fresh ([Claude Code](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). *Support for the loop itself is practitioner experience, not trials.* The two habits added in October have more direct support: independent tests catch more faults ([Konstantinou et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05139)), and an explanation gate cut AI-off maintenance failures from 77% to 39% ([Sankaranarayanan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20206)). | [resources/safe-loop.md](../resources/safe-loop.md) · [prompt patterns](../resources/prompt-patterns.md) |
| **A hint-only course tutor**, kept narrow: the only AI allowed in the Debug Clinic, and optional elsewhere | Unrestricted AI raised practice scores 48% but cut exam scores 17%; a hint-only tutor raised practice 127% with no significant exam harm ([Bastani et al.](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)). CS50's Duck and CodeAid follow the same idea in programming ([Liu et al.](https://cs.harvard.edu/malan/publications/V1fp0567-liu.pdf); [CodeAid](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3613904.3642773)). In October 2026, a 2,379-student trial found that a course-integrated AI tutor *lowered* final grades by 0.37 standard deviations and platform participation by 0.90, most for first-generation students ([EdWorkingPapers](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1598)), so the course does not add a course-wide tutor. *Bastani studied math, the new trial's tutor design is not described, and this tutor is untested in this course.* | [instructor/course-tutor.md](../instructor/course-tutor.md) · [Week 4](../weeks/04-read-debug-own-it/) |
| **The Game in an Hour** in week 2 | Adapted from CMU's one-hour game homework ([Student Wisdom report](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~113/bestPractices.html)). Games give instant visual feedback and need no backend. In Fall 2026 CMU turned it into a 30-minute in-class sprint followed by up to four hours at home, and satisfaction and confidence rose ([CMU F26 report](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~113/bestPracticesF26.html)); this course keeps the hour in Studio A and continues the game in Studio B and the homework. ## What we deliberately did not do

**A course built around one vendor.** Stanford's Fall 2025 course featured one partner tool per week. Its week 2 relied on a free year of Cursor Pro that has since closed to new sign-ups, so that week can no longer be reproduced as written ([report](landscape-report-2026-09.md#existing-courses-start-with-vibe-coding-then-hand-students-more-autonomous-tools)). Vendor academies are free but tied to one tool, and some need a paid plan ([Anthropic Academy](https://anthropic.skilljar.com/claude-code-101)). This course names products only where a step needs them and keeps the specifics in [TOOLS.md](../TOOLS.md).

**AI detectors.** The course *requires* AI, so the question is never "did you use it?" but "can you explain what you shipped?" Educators are moving away from trying to make assignments AI-proof ([ACM Task Force](https://acm-education-genai-task-force.github.io/ACM_Taskforce_GenAI_Report_16Feb26.pdf)). Effort-graded labs, `PROMPTS.md` logs, AI-free writing and oral walkthroughs make detection unnecessary.

**Required paid tools.** The report found that if a course *must* require one paid tier, the evidence supports a cheap editor-assistant upgrade or one month of a frontier agent timed to weeks 6–8. We made both optional ([Syllabus §10](../SYLLABUS.md#10-tools-and-cost)), because the free path works, and learners criticize courses that require paid subscriptions ([Coursera reviews](https://www.coursera.org/learn/claude-code/reviews)).

**Agents in week 1.** Agents amplify whatever habits a student already has ([DORA](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report)), and understanding dropped when agents took over at CMU ([Student Wisdom report](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~113/bestPractices.html)). The destructive-agent incidents in the [case studies](../resources/case-studies.md) show what an agent does without git, tests and containment. We borrowed the useful part of early agent teaching, showing how an agent works from the inside, but placed it in week 6 as [Be the Agent](../weeks/06-agents/be-the-agent/), with no AI at all.

**Cursor or Gemini CLI as the primary tools.** Both were strong choices in 2025. By mid-2026, Cursor's free plan lacked MCP and its free student year had closed, and Gemini CLI's free Google login had ended, while its own README still advertised the old quota for months ([report](landscape-report-2026-09.md#free-tiers-still-exist-in-2026-but-they-are-capped-age-restricted-and-change-monthly); dates in [TOOLS.md](../TOOLS.md)). Copilot Free kept agent mode, MCP and a CLI at no cost, with no age restriction. Since October 2026, Cursor's free plan has been folded into a social-network subscription bundle ([dates in TOOLS.md](../TOOLS.md)).

**Banning outside AI in favor of a course tutor only.** CS50 allows only its own tutor ([CS50](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/2026/honesty/)). That suits a course whose goal is learning to program by hand. This course's goal is learning to direct AI tools safely, so AI is required, and the hint-only tutor is used only where the goal is the student's own thinking: the Debug Clinic.

**A course-wide AI tutor.** It would be easy to add one to every lab. In a 2026 trial with 2,379 students, a course-integrated tutor lowered final grades and participation, most for first-generation students ([EdWorkingPapers](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1598)). The course adds friction for everyone (check first, teach-backs, AI-off tasks, delayed recall) rather than more AI help for those who struggle.

**Grading on whether the app runs.** A working demo is required for the capstone but earns little on its own. Robustness, specs, tests, security and explanation carry the grade, following [Utah](https://github.com/utah-cs3960-sp26/syllabus/blob/main/syllabus.md) and [Michigan](https://eecs498-aase.github.io/syllabus.html).

## Open questions and how this course measures itself

### What nobody knows yet

The report is explicit about the gaps ([report](landscape-report-2026-09.md#ai-raises-output-but-lowers-understanding-unless-teachers-add-structure); [conclusion](landscape-report-2026-09.md#conclusion)):

- **No controlled study has compared course orderings.** The shared shape of existing courses rests on design rationale and student surveys ([Geng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05898)).
- **No semester-long randomized trial has tested teaching non-programmers through vibe coding.** Studies of novices vibe coding are small (19 students in [Geng et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.22614)) or surveys. The longest retention measures in the 2026 studies are 48 hours and one week ([Bergh et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21194); [ICER 2026](https://icer2026.acm.org/track/icer-2026-papers)).
- **No study has tested whether writing specs or tests first helps novices learn.** The case is practitioner consensus, plus one 2026 study about AI-written tests, not learners ([Konstantinou et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05139)).
- **No study shows how much programming knowledge non-programmers need to vibe-code safely.** The [six skills](../resources/without-ai-skills.md) are an expert-consensus floor, not a measured one.
- **The best evidence for hint-only tutors is from high-school math** ([Bastani et al.](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)), and the largest 2026 trial found a course-integrated tutor harmful without describing its design ([EdWorkingPapers](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1598)). The course tutor is an untested adaptation.
- **Productivity evidence is contested.** METR's 2025 and 2026 studies point in different directions, METR calls its 2026 estimates unreliable ([METR 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)), and it has published nothing since May 2026. Professionals' trust is conditional: in Stack Overflow's 2026 survey only 6.6% trusted AI for important decisions ([Stack Overflow](https://stackoverflow.co/company/press/archive/stack-overflow-annual-developer-survey-ai-trust-conditional)).
- **How much non-programmers retain** is the biggest unknown. The course's own delayed-recall questions and week 7 AI-off task can start producing that evidence ([update report](landscape-update-2026-10.md#conclusion)).

### How the course checks itself

Because of those gaps, the course measures its own students' understanding before and after, as the report recommends.

| Measure | What it tells you | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre/post self-assessment** against the eight learning outcomes | How students' confidence changes over the course | [assessment/self-assessment.md](../assessment/self-assessment.md) |
| **Exit tickets**, weekly and AI-free, with one delayed-recall question about last week's project | Which of the six skills are shaky, week by week, and what students still remember a week later | [assessment/exit-tickets.md](../assessment/exit-tickets.md) |
| **The week 7 AI-off maintenance task** on each student's own capstone | Whether students can change their own AI-built code without the AI: the measure on which the 2026 studies found the biggest gaps | [Week 7](../weeks/07-security-and-review/) |

## How to update the course

1. **Tool changes go in [TOOLS.md](../TOOLS.md) only.** That is the point of the [two-layer rule](../CONTRIBUTING.md#the-two-layer-rule). If a concept page needs changing because a tool changed, the page probably named something it shouldn't have.
2. **Before each cohort, and again at mid-course, actually use every primary and fallback tool** instead of trusting the docs; Google's own pages lagged its changes by months. The steps are in [instructor/pre-cohort-checklist.md](../instructor/pre-cohort-checklist.md), and TOOLS.md keeps a calendar of announced changes (in late 2026: Supabase grants on 30 October, Antigravity's models on 2 November, Supabase's legacy keys at the end of the year; [report P5](landscape-update-2026-10.md#priority-5-put-nine-dated-changes-on-the-instructor-calendar)).
3. **Check age and region rules** for the cohort and pick a stack from [instructor/variants.md](../instructor/variants.md) before enrollment.
4. **New evidence** goes into the [research notes](notes/) with a link, then into this page if it changes a decision. Keep every claim linked to a source.
5. **New incidents** can be added to the [case studies](../resources/case-studies.md) only with sources, and conflicting reports should be flagged.
6. **Report problems** through the process in [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md), and run its checks before sending a pull request.
