# Project Fair

> The closing event of the course. A gallery-walk (science-fair) format so everyone presents and
> everyone sees each other's work, plus a few spotlight demos and peer voting.
> Runs about 35 minutes in the studio; give it more time if you can.

## The idea

Not everyone presenting to a silent room. Instead: **half the class presents at stations while the
other half walks around and tries their apps, then they swap.** Two rounds means everyone both
presents and circulates. It's low-pressure, hands-on, and every project gets real users.

## What each presenter brings

- Their **live app** open on their laptop (or a phone), ready to hand to a visitor to try.
- A one-line problem statement they can say in 10 seconds.
- Their **demo video** queued as a backup if the live app hiccups.
- A small sign or sticky note with the app name and its URL/QR code.

## Format

### Two rounds (gallery walk)

- **Round A (12 min):** Group 1 stands at their stations; Group 2 circulates, tries apps, asks
  questions, fills feedback cards, and votes.
- **Swap (1 min).**
- **Round B (12 min):** Group 2 presents; Group 1 circulates.

Presenters give a **60-90 second** pitch when a visitor arrives (problem → quick demo → one thing
they're proud of), then **hand the app over** and let the visitor try it.

### Spotlight demos (8-10 min)

Pick **3-4** projects to demo to the whole room for ~2 minutes each. Choose a spread: one strong
build, one great recovery story, one unusual idea, one that shows a security/RLS feature. Rotate who
gets picked across cohorts so it isn't always the same students.

## Run sheet

| Time | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:03 | Set up stations; split into Group 1 (present first) and Group 2 | Instructor |
| 0:03-0:05 | Explain format, hand out feedback cards and voting slips | Instructor |
| 0:05-0:17 | **Round A:** Group 1 presents, Group 2 circulates + votes | All |
| 0:17-0:18 | Swap | All |
| 0:18-0:30 | **Round B:** Group 2 presents, Group 1 circulates + votes | All |
| 0:30-0:40 | **Spotlight demos** (3-4 projects, ~2 min each) | Selected |
| 0:40-0:45 | Tally votes, announce categories, quick close | Instructor |

> [!TIP]
> For a class larger than ~16, run three rotations of thirds instead of two halves, or hold the Fair
> across two studio slots. Keep each presenter's audience small so apps actually get used.

## Peer voting categories

Give each student a few votes (sticky dots or a slip). Vote for projects other than your own.
Suggested categories — pick 4-5:

- **Most useful** — I'd actually use this.
- **Best recovery story** — the best "what broke and how I fixed it".
- **Most secure** — clearest access rules / thought about safety (great tie to week 7).
- **Best spec** — the app matches a clear, well-written SPEC.
- **Crowd favorite** — overall.
- (Optional) **Most accessible**, **Best README**, **Most surprising**.

Keep it celebratory, not competitive: everyone who ships gets recognized; the categories spotlight
different strengths so many students win something.

## Feedback cards

Print a stack; each visitor fills one per app they try. Keep them short:

```
App: ____________________   Visitor: ____________

I tried: (what you did in the app)
________________________________________________

One thing I liked:
________________________________________________

One thing I'd improve:
________________________________________________

Did it work from the app alone (no help)?   ☐ yes  ☐ mostly  ☐ no
```

Presenters keep their cards — they're honest user feedback and good material for the final
reflection.

## Accessibility considerations

- **Stations:** leave clear paths for wheelchair and mobility access; don't cram tables.
- **Noise:** a loud room is hard for some people. Offer a quieter corner and let anyone present their
  video with headphones instead of a live pitch.
- **Presenting is optional in form:** a student who can't do a live spoken pitch may play their demo
  video and answer questions in writing or chat.
- **Standing:** provide chairs; nobody has to stand for the whole event.
- **Visuals:** ask presenters to zoom their app to a readable size; remind everyone that color-only
  cues are hard to see.
- **Feedback cards:** offer a digital version (a form) for anyone who prefers typing.

## Online/hybrid variant

Run the same shape in a video call:

- **Stations = breakout rooms.** Group 1 each host a breakout room with their screen shared; Group 2
  members drop into rooms, watch a quick demo, try it via the live URL, and fill a **digital feedback
  form**. Swap for Round B.
- **Spotlights** happen in the main room with screen sharing.
- **Voting** via a poll or a shared form.
- Ask presenters to share the **live URL in chat** so visitors interact directly, not just watch.
- Record only with everyone's consent; keep cameras optional.

For a **hybrid** room (some in person, some remote), pair each remote presenter with an in-person
buddy who can carry a laptop to their station, and put all URLs/QR codes in a shared doc so both
audiences can reach every app.

## After the Fair

- Announce category winners; thank everyone for shipping.
- Remind students the **final submission** and **oral walkthrough 3** are still due
  ([homework.md](homework.md), [oral-walkthroughs.md](../../assessment/oral-walkthroughs.md#walkthrough-3-week-8)).
- Encourage students to keep their repos public as a portfolio and to follow the
  [Keep going roadmap](homework.md#keep-going-roadmap-after-the-course).
